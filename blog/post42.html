The Eye That Never Blinks: Kiri's Take on Zao's Okama

The earth, looking back

by Kiri

My sister was making a racket again, wasn't she. Fire dragon, water dragon, a place where the two finally meet…… always the romantic, that one.

……But that thing she said. "The earth's eye." I don't think she was entirely wrong, actually.

We climb our mountains and stand at the rim, convinced we're the ones looking down into the crater. But picture it the way she described — the barren, ruined rock around it as an eyelid, and that still, round water at the center as an eye. The eye itself. Not a metaphor for one. The eye.

Maybe we're not looking down at the Okama at all. Maybe the earth has been looking straight through us this whole time, past us, out toward whatever's waiting on the other side of the sky.

A Color That Speaks to the Sun

This lake people call the "Okama" isn't just a pretty patch of green water, either. It has minerals dissolved in it, and depending on the sunlight, the weather, the season — the surface shifts from emerald green to a deep lapis blue. That's exactly why it carries other names too, ones that translate roughly to "the five-colored lake."

Scientists call it optical magic — sunlight scattering off suspended particles in the water, nothing more. But people who lived near this mountain long before that explanation existed saw it differently. They believed the color change wasn't a trick of the light at all, but the mood of a sleeping dragon underground, or a message rising up from the earth itself.

The crater is thought to have formed in an eruption roughly 800 years ago, and even now, volcanic gas still escapes from the lakebed — this is very much a living volcano, not a dormant memory of one. The water is strongly acidic, hostile to any life at all. And yet it changes color as if it were speaking to the sun.

……There's something that gives me a small chill, thinking about that.

When the Eye Closes

Apparently the odds of seeing the Okama clearly from the shrine at the summit are only around thirty percent. Travelers call it lucky when the clouds part in time. But I like to think that when it's hidden, the earth has simply closed its eye for a while, resting.

That third poster my sister showed you last — the one holding all that strange emerald water. I won't pretend it's for everyone. But for the right person, I think it lands hard.

A cold that keeps everything at a distance, and a heat rising from somewhere far below the surface — two things that shouldn't sit together, sharing the same single sheet of paper. Nothing flashy about it. But look at it long enough, and you start to feel pulled in, toward whatever's at the bottom.

If what you're looking for isn't just a nice view, but something a little unresolved, a little unexplained —

……hang this on your wall, and I think your life might shift, just slightly. There are worse ways to spend your days than under the earth's quiet, watching gaze.

No need to rush.

Take your time, and choose whichever one actually reaches you.

We'll be right here. Same as always, waiting quietly.

……Nya.

Kiri
