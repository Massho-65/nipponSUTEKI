Walking Through Japan’s “Land of the Dead” — Mount Osore

by Kiri

To be honest, I was a little afraid before coming here.

People call this place “the closest place to the afterlife in Japan.”

Mount Osore sits quietly in the far north of Aomori, hidden deep in the mountains of the Shimokita Peninsula.

The moment I stepped off the bus, the smell of sulfur filled the air.

Like a hot spring town — and yet, much quieter. Almost too quiet.

A Landscape Like Another World

Gray rocks. Cracked ground. Smoke drifting from the earth.

The place looks almost like Mars.

This area is known as Jigokudani — Hell Valley, and it truly feels like stepping into another realm.

If someone told me this was what the world after death looked like, I think I would simply believe them.

Everything is silent. Still. Timeless.

From Hell to Paradise in Five Minutes

But then, something unexpected happens.

Walk just a few minutes further, and the harsh volcanic scenery suddenly opens into a calm lake.

  • White sand
    Soft and bright under the sky
  • Emerald water
    Quiet waves touching the shore

This is Gokurakuhama — Paradise Beach.

Heaven and hell exist side by side here. Only a short walk apart.

A Place for the Dead — and the Living

Many spiritual places around the world feel energetic and lively.

But Mount Osore feels different.

This is not a place to gain power or make wishes. It is a place to remember those who have passed away.

People come here to pray, to talk quietly to loved ones, and to face grief with gentle acceptance.

Strangely, there are even hot springs here — people bathing peacefully beside this sacred ground.

Life and death coexist naturally, without fear. And maybe that is something uniquely Japanese.

Kiri

Standing by the lake, I didn’t feel scared at all. If this is the place closest to the afterlife, maybe death isn’t darkness — maybe it’s just quiet, like this wind and water.

Mount Osore doesn’t shout or try to impress you.

It whispers.

Through silence, through wind, through empty space.

And somehow, those quiet places stay with you the longest.

If you ever travel to Japan, take a step away from the crowded cities and come here.

Walk slowly. Breathe deeply. Listen.

You might discover that the boundary between life and death is softer than you ever imagined.
